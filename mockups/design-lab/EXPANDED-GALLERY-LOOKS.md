# Expanded Design Lab Gallery — Looks #5, #7, #8 and #9

Version `0.8.0` promotes every previously deferred numbered Look into the active Design Lab gallery. The user explicitly chose not to eliminate visual directions at this stage.

All four additions use the same immutable fixture, three comparison screens, seven scenarios, routes, capture modes, and simulated actions as the existing gallery.

## Look #5 — Playful Modular

**Emotional target:** Friendly, optimistic, and easy to begin.

**Visual system:** Color-blocked cards, rounded modular controls, small symbols, chunky outlines, and cheerful but controlled accents.

**Strengths:** Makes chores feel less severe; supports clear grouping; approachable for new users and household use.

**Risks:** Too much color could become juvenile or visually noisy in dense professional and administrative Areas.

**Intervention:** A friendly invitation to swap screens for one small action. Staying remains explicitly acceptable.

## Look #7 — Bold Utility

**Emotional target:** Decisive, clear, and energetic.

**Visual system:** Heavy black rules, oversized headings, cream surfaces, red/yellow/blue accents, compact data rows, and square controls.

**Strengths:** Extremely scannable; distinctive; strong under Heavy Backlog and Large Household conditions.

**Risks:** Can feel demanding or confrontational if the direct visual language is not balanced by humane copy.

**Intervention:** A direct context-switch prompt that keeps all three choices available without punishment.

## Look #8 — Ambient Glass

**Emotional target:** Calm, modern, spacious, and emotionally light.

**Visual system:** Translucent cards, atmospheric gradients, soft depth, cool neutral typography, and gentle focus cues.

**Strengths:** Premium and calming; strong emotional fit for reflection, intervention, and low-pressure upkeep.

**Risks:** Blur and translucency can weaken contrast, reduce density, or perform inconsistently on lower-end devices.

**Intervention:** A quiet invitation to change pace, with an explicit no-penalty option to remain in the current app.

## Look #9 — Retro Digital

**Emotional target:** A satisfying, friendly home operating system.

**Visual system:** Dark panels, segmented counters, terminal-style labels, green/amber status lights, scan-line texture, and monospaced hierarchy.

**Strengths:** Highly distinctive; excellent status visibility; useful for users who enjoy systems, dashboards, and progress instrumentation.

**Risks:** Can feel technical, niche, or less emotionally warm; small monospaced labels require careful accessibility review.

**Intervention:** An optional mode switch presented like a system prompt rather than a moral warning.

## Gallery rule

No Look is rejected merely because another Look is stronger overall. Future work may:

- Keep a pure Look for a complete prototype.
- Apply a Look to a specific product surface where it performs best.
- Build controlled synthesis variants with one dominant foundation and explicitly documented borrowed components.

Unrestricted mixing within a single screen remains prohibited because it would destroy visual coherence and make evaluation meaningless.

## Next quality work

The four new Looks have initial responsive, Large Text, long-content, forced-colors, and reduced-motion foundations. They still require the same dedicated code-level quality passes previously completed for Looks #2, #3, #4, and #6, followed by browser and device evidence.
