# Look #2 — Warm Editorial

## Design intent

Warm Editorial presents Nudge as a calm record of places and routines rather than a conventional task manager. It should feel thoughtful, practical, and composed without becoming nostalgic, decorative, or exclusively household-oriented.

## Core principles

1. **Lead with language and hierarchy.** Typography, spacing, and editorial rules establish structure before cards or decoration.
2. **Treat urgency as information, not alarm.** Terracotta and mustard identify overdue and due-today work without turning the screen into a warning dashboard.
3. **Use fewer containers.** Flat lists, rules, and generous margins should replace unnecessary nested cards.
4. **Keep one strong reading path.** The eye should move from page idea, to attention summary, to Area index, to next action.
5. **Allow useful density.** Warmth must not require excessive whitespace or hide practical metadata.
6. **Make interventions humane.** The interruption acknowledges time spent without judgment and offers a small action with clear escape routes.

## Palette roles

- **Warm ivory:** Primary app surface and reading background
- **Charcoal-green:** Main text, navigation, and strongest actions
- **Olive:** Neutral status, labels, and calm completion language
- **Terracotta:** Overdue state and selective editorial emphasis
- **Muted mustard:** Due-today state
- **Dusty blue:** Optional secondary information, not currently dominant

## Typography

- **Fraunces:** Display headings and selected Area or Section names
- **Inter:** Body copy, actions, and practical row text
- **JetBrains Mono:** Kicker labels, dates, counts, build metadata, and compact statuses

Serif typography should establish identity without being used for dense supporting metadata.

## Layout rules

- Use editorial headers rather than conventional app bars in the audition.
- Use thin horizontal rules to separate conceptual sections.
- Prefer full-width flat rows for Areas and routines.
- Keep status counts aligned consistently at the trailing edge.
- Allow long content to wrap; do not silently hide the design-system stress case.
- Use cards only for a singular highlighted object, such as the intervention suggestion or an empty-state poster.

## Icon approach

Round 1 intentionally relies on typography and small status marks rather than illustrative room icons. Later rounds may introduce simple line icons, but they should:

- Use consistent stroke weight
- Avoid cartoon room illustrations
- Remain secondary to labels
- Work equally for Home, Car, Personal, and Work

## Motion and completion feedback

Later interactive work should use restrained motion:

- Short fades and slight vertical movement
- No celebratory confetti
- A completed row may soften, briefly acknowledge completion, then move according to product rules
- Light, Moderate, and Deep grading should feel like selecting an editorial stamp, not earning a game reward
- Respect reduced-motion settings

## Forms and sheets for Round 2

- Favor calm full-page or bottom-sheet forms with clear labeled sections
- Keep Repeat and First Due visually primary for Chores
- Place Duration and completion grading under More Options
- Use terracotta only for destructive actions and overdue information
- Confirmation copy should be plain and reversible where possible

## Versatility beyond the home

Warm Editorial must support:

- **Car:** Maintenance log and recurring care
- **Personal:** Health, administration, and personal routines
- **Work:** Desk, planning, digital workspace, and recurring professional upkeep

Avoid wording such as “your home” when the parent concept is Areas. Home-specific language may appear only within Home Areas.

## Anti-patterns

- Excessive paper textures, torn edges, or scrapbook effects
- Decorative serif type in every row
- Vintage or farmhouse visual clichés
- Hiding counts or due states for aesthetic purity
- Oversized whitespace that makes routine-heavy Areas inefficient
- Making Work and Personal appear like afterthoughts
- Using red warning language for ordinary overdue chores
- Intervention copy that sounds parental, disappointed, or moralizing

## Round 1 evaluation question

Can Warm Editorial make Nudge feel distinctive and calm while remaining fast, scalable, and credible for Home, Car, Personal, and Work?
